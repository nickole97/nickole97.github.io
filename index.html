About Me

I'm a 4th-year PhD candidate at the University of California, Irvine, working in the Hammer Lab. My research focuses on understanding the evolutionary dynamics of gut microbiomes in neotropical bees, particularly how host social behavior and group size influence the maintenance and loss of specialized microbial symbionts.

Originally from Bogotá, Colombia, I completed my undergraduate studies at Universidad de los Andes, where I developed my passion for microbial ecology and tropical biology. My work combines field biology, molecular techniques, and computational approaches to explore questions at the intersection of microbiology, evolution, and ecology.

Beyond research, I'm passionate about science communication and creating spaces for knowledge sharing. I've organized workshops and discussion groups to make microbial ecology accessible to diverse audiences.

Current Research

My dissertation research investigates how social organization affects the evolution of gut microbiomes in stingless bees (Meliponini). Using phylogenetic comparative methods and metagenomic analyses, I'm uncovering patterns of symbiont gain and loss across bee diversification and exploring how host group size mediates the maintenance of specialized gut bacteria.

Current Projects

  • Symbiont evolution in stingless bees
  • Microbiome disruption in neotropical bumblebees
  • Comparative genomics of bee-associated bacteria
  • Fieldwork in Latin American bee populations

Methods & Approaches

  • 16S rRNA amplicon sequencing
  • Metagenomic & genomic analysis
  • Phylogenetic comparative methods
  • Field sampling & specimen collection
