About Me

I'm a PhD candidate at the University of California, Irvine, working in the Hammer Lab. Originally from Bogotá, Colombia, I completed my undergraduate studies at Universidad de los Andes, where I developed my passion for microbial ecology and tropical biology.

My research combines field biology, molecular techniques, and computational approaches to explore questions at the intersection of microbiology, evolution, and ecology. I'm particularly interested in how social structure and environmental gradients shape the evolutionary dynamics of host-microbe symbioses in neotropical bees.

Beyond research, I'm passionate about science communication and creating spaces for knowledge sharing. I've organized workshops and discussion groups to make microbial ecology accessible to diverse audiences.

Current Research

Among social bees, does colony size determine which symbionts persist across evolutionary time?

Working with stingless bees (Meliponini), I used phylogenetic comparative methods to test whether social structure—specifically colony size—predicts the long-term maintenance or loss of specialized gut bacteria.

I found that larger colonies are significantly more likely to retain core symbionts like Snodgrassella and Gilliamella, revealing how social organization can mediate symbiont stability over millions of years of host diversification.

How do gut microbiomes evolve when hosts colonize new environments?

Bumblebees (Bombus) independently dispersed from temperate zones into tropical mountains multiple times, losing winter diapause and encountering new thermal regimes and floral resources. In 2023, we published initial work showing that gut microbiome composition remained surprisingly conserved across these climatic transitions.

Now, using shotgun metagenomics on newly collected samples from Colombia and the USA, I'm investigating whether these transitions drove convergent changes at finer scales—specifically in microbial strain diversity and functional gene repertoires. This work tests whether microbiomes facilitate or constrain host adaptation to new climates.
